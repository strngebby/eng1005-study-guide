## What a vector is

A **scalar** is a size only, such as 5 m. A **vector** has a size and a direction, such as 5 m north-east. In 3D we write it as three moves along x, y and z.

<Eq>{`v = (v₁, v₂, v₃) = v₁i + v₂j + v₃k`}</Eq>

i, j and k are the unit vectors along the three axes.

<Figure3D {...FIG.comp} />

## Adding, scaling, length

Add, subtract and scale one component at a time. A negative multiple reverses the direction.

<Eq>{`|v| = √(v₁² + v₂² + v₃²)
unit vector:  v̂ = v / |v|
from A to B:  AB = b − a`}</Eq>

<Callout label="Watch out">You can never divide by a vector, and a scalar can never equal a vector.</Callout>

<Figure3D {...FIG.add} />

## Direction cosines

If θ, φ and ψ are the angles v makes with i, j and k, then the unit vector lists their cosines.

<Eq>{`v̂ = (cos θ, cos φ, cos ψ)
cos²θ + cos²φ + cos²ψ = 1`}</Eq>

So if you know two angles, the third is fixed apart from a ± sign. Check whether it is acute or obtuse.

## Dot product

Takes two vectors and gives a **number**. Use it for angles, perpendicularity and shadows.

<Eq>{`a·b = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃ = |a||b| cos θ
a·a = |a|²          a ⟂ b  ⇔  a·b = 0  (non-zero a, b)
scalar projection of b on a = b·â
vector projection          = (b·â) â`}</Eq>

<Figure3D {...FIG.dot} />

## Cross product

Takes two vectors and gives a **vector** perpendicular to both. The right-hand rule picks which way it points.

<Eq>{`a × b = (a₂b₃ − a₃b₂,  a₃b₁ − a₁b₃,  a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)
|a × b| = |a||b| sin θ = parallelogram area
triangle area = ½ |AB × AC|
b × a = −(a × b)      parallel vectors give 0`}</Eq>

Check your answer: its dot product with a and with b must both be 0.

<Figure3D {...FIG.cross} />

## Scalar triple product

The volume of a parallelepiped is base area times height, which becomes one expression.

<Eq>{`volume = |a·(b × c)|
a·(b × c) = b·(c × a) = c·(a × b)`}</Eq>

A result of 0 means the three vectors lie in one plane.

<Figure3D {...FIG.triple} />

## Lines

A line needs a point a and a direction b. Through two points P and Q, use b = Q − P. Any non-zero multiple of b describes the same line.

<Eq>{`vector:      x(s) = a + s b
parametric:  x = a₁+sb₁,  y = a₂+sb₂,  z = a₃+sb₃
symmetric:   (x−a₁)/b₁ = (y−a₂)/b₂ = (z−a₃)/b₃   (all bᵢ ≠ 0)`}</Eq>

**Is a point on the line?** All three equations must give the same s.

<Figure3D {...FIG.line} />

## Two lines: how are they related?

Give the lines **different parameters** (s and t).

| Directions parallel? | Common point? | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yes | Yes | Same line |
| Yes | No | Parallel |
| No | Yes | Intersecting |
| No | No | Skew |

To test for a common point, solve two component equations, then check the third.

<Eq>{`skew:      n = b₁ × b₂,   d = |(a₂ − a₁)·n| / |n|
parallel:  d = |(a₂ − a₁) × b| / |b|
point P to line a + sb:   d = |(P − a) × b| / |b|`}</Eq>

<Figure3D {...FIG.skew} />

## Planes

A plane can be built from a point and two non-parallel directions, or from a point and a normal.

<Eq>{`parametric:  x(s,t) = a + s b₁ + t b₂
vector:      x·n = a·n,   n = b₁ × b₂
Cartesian:   n₁x + n₂y + n₃z = d
distance from origin = |d| / |n|`}</Eq>

**Line meets plane:** substitute x(s), y(s), z(s) into the Cartesian equation and solve for s. No solution means the line is parallel and off the plane; every s works means it lies in the plane.

Three planes may meet at one point, along a line, or have no common point.

## Which tool do I use?

| You want | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Angle or perpendicular test | Dot product |
| Normal or area | Cross product |
| Volume or coplanar test | Triple product |
| Where things meet | Set equations equal, or substitute |
| Shortest distance | Project onto the perpendicular direction |
